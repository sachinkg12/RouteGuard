IT ticket routing with selective prediction

Know when not to answer.

RouteGuard tests whether a classifier should auto-route an IT support ticket or defer it to a human when a wrong route costs more than triage. It reports accuracy, calibration, coverage, and expected cost—but does not claim a new abstention algorithm.

02 · Study at a glance

The evidence, before the headline.

A compact map of what was evaluated, how leakage was controlled, and where the conclusions stop.

Corpus47,837 tickets

Eight IT support categories from the public IT Service Ticket Classification Dataset, version 1 (CC0).

Split70 / 10 / 20

Stratified train, calibration, and test partitions. Few-shot examples come from training only.

Models4 + 8 settings

Four trained non-LLM baselines plus eight prompted settings across four LLM families (zero- and few-shot). LLM evaluation uses a stratified 1,000-ticket test subset to bound API cost.

ProtocolSelect, then test

Non-LLM thresholds are selected on calibration and evaluated once on test. The reported LLM policy uses a fixed cost-derived threshold, never a test-selected one.

03 · The cost problem

Accuracy hides the expensive mistakes.

Two systems with similar accuracy can have different operational value when wrong routes and human review have different consequences. RouteGuard evaluates the route-or-defer decision under an explicit cost model.

E[cost]
=
(1 − coverage) × 1deferred → human triage + coverage × (1 − routed acc) × 5auto-routed but wrong + 0auto-routed and correct

Coverage is the share of tickets auto-routed. Routed accuracy is accuracy among only those tickets. Raising the threshold usually trades coverage for fewer wrong routes.

Plain-language metric guide
Abstention
Letting the system decline an automatic decision and ask a human.
Calibration
How closely stated confidence matches observed correctness on average.
ECE
A binned calibration summary. A lower ECE does not by itself prove that confidence ranks correct cases above incorrect ones.
04 · How it works

A pipeline that separates concerns.

Prediction, confidence, abstention, and cost are separate stages. Predictor implementations are registry-based and do not require evaluator changes. Confidence methods, policies, datasets, and clients currently require explicit builder or policy-set wiring.

Predictorlabel + score
Confidenceestimate or recalibrate
Abstentionroute or defer
Cost modelexplicit assumptions
Evaluatortables + figures
Predictors

Classical & LLM

TF-IDF (LR / SVM / RF), fine-tuned DistilBERT, and prompted LLMs share one prediction interface.

Confidence

Several signals

Model-reported score, cross-predictor agreement, and optional held-out isotonic recalibration.

Abstention

Threshold policies

Route above a displayed threshold and defer below it; always-route and always-defer are reference policies.

Cost

Explicit & configurable

The framework supports configurable cost triples. The paper reports the illustrative (0, 1, 5) setting and threshold sensitivity.

06 · Findings

What was measured—and on which model

Public 47,837-ticket corpus, eight classes. Default illustrative cost model (c_ok, c_triage, c_wrong) = (0, 1, 5).

38.1%lower test cost for TF-IDF + LR at calibration-selected τ = 0.60; 95% CI 35.7–40.4%
0.477best prompted-LLM accuracy: Haiku few-shot on the common 1,000-ticket subset
0.877best non-LLM accuracy: fine-tuned DistilBERT
95.0%TF-IDF + LR routed accuracy at τ = 0.60
70.5%TF-IDF + LR coverage at τ = 0.60
3.5%TF-IDF + LR wrong-route rate at τ = 0.60
Calibration caveat

Isotonic recalibration lowers reported ECE, but ECE alone does not show whether confidence retains useful ranking ability. The camera-ready analysis therefore reports correctness AUROC and tie-aware AURC beside ECE; GPT-4o-mini few-shot remains at correctness AUROC 0.500.

LLM deployment-readiness result

At the analytically derived break-even threshold τ = 0.80, seven of eight prompted settings route 0 of 1,000 tickets. Haiku few-shot routes 19 of 1,000 (1.9%), all correctly, for cost 0.981 versus 1.000 for defer-all. This is a low-coverage observation, not evidence of broad auto-routing readiness.

Cost reductions of 35.5–38.1% were reported across four non-LLM baselines under the default cost assumption. These are dataset- and cost-specific measurements, not general production guarantees.

09 · Limitations

What this study does not establish.

One corpus

Results on one public ticket dataset do not establish performance across organizations or domains.

Illustrative costs

The default (0, 1, 5) model is hypothetical; deployment requires organization-specific costs.

Threshold-grid sensitivity

Primary operating points are the best over five predeclared thresholds. A 0.01-spaced DistilBERT sensitivity sweep moves its calibration choice from 0.90 to 0.94 without changing the conclusion.

Limited policy comparison

Top probability, top-two margin, and inverse entropy are compared, but no jointly trained learning-to-defer method is evaluated.

Calibration is not discrimination

A low ECE can coexist with an uninformative or nearly constant confidence score.

Prompt and taxonomy access

Prompted LLMs receive category names and at most three examples per class, but no label definitions; trained baselines learn label conventions from 33,486 examples.

Agreement-pool confounding

The main M=9 agreement pool includes supervised TF-IDF + LR. The paper separately reports an LLM-only M=8 control so peer consensus is not conflated with the mixed pool.

Near-duplicate audit

At cosine similarity ≥ 0.90, 8.33% of the corpus has a near twin and 7.05% of the random test split has a training near twin. A neighbor-group refit changes TF-IDF + LR accuracy from 0.8474 to 0.8500; a direct audit still finds 26 of 14,458 group-test tickets (0.18%) with a group-train near twin.

Offline evidence only

This is not a deployed service, human user study, or guarantee against provider and model drift.
